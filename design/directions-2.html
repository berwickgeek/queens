Queens: three more directions

Same 8x8 puzzle and position as the first page, three new looks. Each takes its whimsy from a craft: glazed tiles, a hand-drawn map, embroidery.

D. Azulejo

Glazed ceramic tiles, Lisbon-style: each region is a different glaze with a soft highlight and grout lines between tiles, on a crisp white-blue page with cobalt ink. Queens are hand-painted cobalt crowns. Whimsy: a placed queen settles with a tiny tile "clink" (a short sharp haptic), and a solved board flips its tiles over in a wave to show a white-and-blue patterned back. Clean and calm, readable at 11x11. Less overtly cute than the others.

E. Storybook map

Each region is a kingdom on a hand-drawn map: watercolour washes that bleed a little at the edges, with wobbly ink borders. Every queen rules one kingdom. Storybook serif titles. Whimsy: puzzles get small invented kingdom names in the picker ("The Marsh of Eight"), the washes deepen as you place queens, and a solve unfurls a little banner over the board. The most charming and the most decorative; the soft edges cost some clarity on the biggest boards.

F. Cross-stitch sampler

Regions are blocks of thread colour stitched on pale cloth, with a dashed backstitch where regions meet. The crosses you mark are literal cross-stitches. Soft rounded type, a dashed "hoop" round the board. Whimsy: marking a cell stitches the X in two quick strokes, queens are embroidered crowns, and a solve finishes the sampler with a stitched border running round the hoop. Cosy and tactile. The texture is the busiest of the six.
